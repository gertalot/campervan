# Two-module cabinet proposal

Implemented design, 7 November 2026. The earlier research is retained below; the following decisions supersede its provisional block-joint details.

## Final joint design

The lower module retains the middle shelves and their 18 × 18mm supports. The upper module has four transverse
40mm-vertical × 18mm-wide Pinetrim untreated pine joining rails, two per bay. There are no upper joining rails
along the door-facing or wall-facing edges. The four former middle-level rear corner blocks are replaced by
the ends of the transverse joining rails.

The rails bear directly over the lower transverse shelf supports. Bolts are on the transverse sides only: two in each bay's
end joining rails, including both sides of the centre divider. This gives eight sets total, positioned 40mm
from the rail ends. There are no bolts on the aisle/door-facing or wall-facing longitudinal rails.
Each bore is 5.5mm, passing through upper
40mm timber, 6mm shelf and lower 18mm timber. Use M5 × 75mm bolts, two 15mm OD × 1mm washers and a nyloc nut per
set. The CAD heads, nuts and washers are clearance envelopes, not supplier machining drawings. Fixing products
and price remain to select in Materials. Do not apply a generic metal-joint tightening torque to the pine.

At the cab-end panel, centre partition and rear upright the lower panel extends 20mm above the shelf. Upper panel
edges start another 0.5mm above this lip. The ring's adjacent bottom faces have a 0.5mm guide relief, extending
20.5mm up from the shelf; above that it bonds flush against the upper panels. Plane/sand or rebate these guide
faces during dry assembly. This gives the 20mm lip a running fit without cutting into the shelf supports.

The complete rear outside panel belongs to the upper module, extending down to its original lower edge at
Z730, below the middle shelf top. There is no lower-module strip or guide lip on that panel.
The front frame's seam stays at the shelf top, with 0.5mm clearance above. There is no front lip obstructing the
horizontal insertion path. The four upper doors belong to the upper module; the five lower doors belong to the
lower module. Their existing hinge origins and dimensions remain. The plastic backing is split too.

The joining ring is scribed at the rear pillar where required by the model. At the shared level the wall rail
position also reserves the ring's full 40mm height, avoiding a new wall collision above the original 60mm rail.
All guides and holes are modelled, but the complete installation path and joint strength still require assessment.
The 18 × 18mm supports elsewhere remain unchanged.

Verified upper stock: [Pinetrim 40 × 18 × 1800mm untreated pine, Bunnings item 0132618](https://www.bunnings.co.nz/pinetrim-40-x-18-x-1-8m-premium-untreated-kiln-dried-pine-dressed-4-sides_p0132618),
listed at NZ$11.50 per length on 7 November 2026; local stock not confirmed.

## Earlier research

## Recommendation

Split at the top face of the shared middle shelves, currently 754mm above the finished floor.
This is the second shelf elevation when counting the lower forward shelf as the first; the rear bay has no shelf
at that lowest elevation. The recommendation assumes this is the level Gert means.

The lower module would be about 754mm high and the upper about 530.5mm high, each retaining the existing 1000mm
overall length. The current assembled carcass's maximum depth is 381mm. Both modules are substantially smaller
than the rear opening with its working 50mm height allowance: 1230mm high. This is an envelope comparison,
not a completed collision-free installation-path calculation.

## Why this exact joint line

The existing middle support rails run from Z730 to Z790. The 18mm ledges occupy Z730–748 and the 6mm shelves
occupy Z748–754. Splitting at Z754 naturally leaves:

- Lower module: existing shelf ledges and both middle shelves, plus a 24mm-high front/rear rail portion.
- Upper module: the remaining 36mm-high rail portions, both upper shelves and the ceiling-following top.
- Existing 36mm-high middle-rail joint blocks entirely within the upper module, rather than cut into small pieces.

The two middle shelves act as the top diaphragms of the lower module and the floor of the assembled upper
compartments. The upper module has no duplicate full bottom sheet. Its lower rail portions, partitions and own
upper shelves provide its handling rigidity; the narrow lower rail portions must remain connected during lifting.

The lower doors stop at Z745 and the adjacent upper doors start at Z775, so a Z754 seam falls within the exposed
frame rail between door rows. Existing door sizes and hinge elevations can remain. The seam will be a visible
fine joint in that rail; it is not automatically hidden by the doors.

A cut at Z760 merely to centre the seam would divide the existing 36mm joint blocks into 6mm and 30mm pieces.
A cut at Z790 would leave the full rail below the joint, but put the next door's nominal hinge line below the upper
module's base. Both alternatives require additional reworking without improving entry clearance meaningfully.

## Proposed detachable joint

Use bolted bearing blocks/cleats around the perimeter, accessed through the open doorways. Start the layout with
four connection stations per bay, eight total, near the aisle-side and wall-side ends of the transverse panels.
This is a proposed layout, not a proven bolt count or capacity.

- Upper-module blocks bond permanently to the upper frame/partitions. Lower receiving ledges or locally widened
  pads bond permanently to the lower module. Bolts join the two after positioning.
- Prefer vertical through-bolts with washers and locking nuts, fitted from within the compartments. Choose diameter,
  length and pad widths together; do not assume the existing 18mm pine section is adequate for every bolt layout.
- Existing rear rail blocks may serve as upper bearing blocks where hole access and edge distances permit.
  Additional local aisle-side blocks/pads will be needed.
- Put each clamped location directly over a supporting ledge/pad. Align upper and lower vertical panel edges for
  direct bearing. Avoid concentrating upper-module weight onto unsupported 6mm shelf material.
- Arrange the connection stations to resist sliding and separation in both horizontal directions. Do not assume
  friction alone replaces a checked shear connection or that an unconnected butt seam reproduces the original frame.
- Assemble the joint dry: no glue between modules. Predrill matching holes during the workshop dry assembly.
  Avoid threaded inserts in 9mm plywood edges and blind wood screws as the principal detachable connection.

Connector bolts and cap nuts/cross dowels are established knock-down furniture mechanisms. Simple through-bolts
are preferable here because both faces can be made accessible and local bearing pads can accommodate thin plywood.
The exact hardware purchase and connection capacities remain to be selected during detailed design.

Plywood splice plates across the seam are another inexpensive option, but plates on inside faces can intersect
the shelf and its ledges and require local notches. Bolted corner blocks avoid running long splice plates through
the existing shelf-support assembly.

## Installation and removal

1. Dry-assemble both modules together outside, align the faces and drill their connection holes. Separate for fitting.
2. Carry in the lower module without doors and place it around the wheel arch. Fit it before the benches obstruct access.
3. Bring the upper module in upright and raise it in the open interior beside its final position.
4. Slide it horizontally onto the lower module at the final elevation, then insert and tighten the connecting bolts.
5. Fit the direct vehicle brackets and install/adjust the doors.

Keep the joint free of preinstalled upward-projecting dowels: a ceiling-height upper module cannot necessarily be
lifted above them and lowered onto them. Matching bolt holes can be aligned with temporary clamps, with bolts fitted
after the module is in place. Any separate alignment keys must permit the horizontal assembly/removal direction.

The contour-following backing also needs a split or removable joint, so it does not permanently bridge the two modules.
Door chains, brackets and other fixings must not accidentally prevent the intended separation.

## Remaining design work

Model the two modules and joint pads, check tool access and the upper module's handling rigidity, and verify each
installation move against the van and wheel arch. Allow joint clearance without reducing the intended full height.
Reassess racking across the detachable seam; the earlier continuous-frame calculations do not establish its stiffness.
Keep module bearing and its detachable joint separate from the cabinet-to-vehicle brackets.
