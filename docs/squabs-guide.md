# Five removable covers for the campervan mattress

Updated 26 October 2026. Dimensions below come from the current CAD parameters and
`src/campervan/cad/cushions.py`. You already own the foam mattress and will cut it yourself.
The model uses 100 mm foam; measure your mattress before cutting either foam or cloth.

## 2.1 — Instructions

Make five covers with two faces, four rectangular sides, a concealed #5 coil zip along one long side, and a small fabric
pocket over the closed zip pull. Use the same cloth on both faces so you can turn the cushions over. Put your fitted
sheet over the assembled bed.

This method hides the zip behind two fabric folds. You will see two lines of stitching alongside them. Use a standard
zipper foot for this construction. The pocket in these steps hides the zip pull.

### Learn the terms you will use while sewing

**Right side:** the fabric surface you want outside. **Wrong side:** the surface against the foam. Put **right sides
together** by placing the outside surfaces against each other. A **raw edge** is an edge you cut. A **seam allowance**
is the cloth between the raw edge and your stitches. Here, you will sew the structural seams 15 mm from the raw edge.

**Boxing:** the four sides of the cover. **Baste:** hold pieces together with tape or temporary long stitches.
**Backstitch:** sew backwards for two or three stitches to secure the end of a seam. **Zigzag:** a stitch that moves
from side to side; you will use it along cut edges to reduce fraying. **Selvedges:** the two finished edges running
along a fabric roll. They may have printing, tiny holes or a stiff strip. Keep those strips outside your pattern pieces
when measuring the usable fabric width.

### Check the mattress you already own

Remove its removable outer cover. Measure the bare foam's usable length, width and thickness with it lying flat and
uncompressed. Record those three measurements here. You need a usable rectangle **1070 × 1860 mm** for the current
design. Check both ends and the middle for thickness: the CAD currently assumes **100 mm**.

Keep any bonded foam layers together while measuring. If the usable rectangle is smaller, or the thickness differs,
update the CAD and these cut dimensions before cutting. Keep the mattress intact until the bench dimensions have passed
your physical mock-up check.

### Mark and cut your mattress into those five pieces

Borrow an electric carving knife and practise on an offcut outside the required rectangle. A NZ purchase option is the
[Sunbeam Carveasy Twin Blade at
Briscoes](https://www.briscoes.co.nz/product/1105964/sunbeam-carveasy-twin-blade-electric-knife/), listed at NZ$49.99 on
sale when checked. This is a kitchen carving knife used for the cutting technique shown by
[OnlineFabricStore](https://www.onlinefabricstore.com/makersmill/how-to-cut-foam/).

Lay the mattress on a supported surface with clearance below the cut. Use a long ruler and square to mark a **1070 ×
1860 mm** rectangle on both faces. Join the marks down the sides. Cut away the surplus outside it.

Measure **1280 mm** from one short end and mark a line across the full 1070 mm width. Cut there. Label the **1070 × 580
mm** piece **Cross seat**.

On the **1070 × 1280 mm** remainder, mark lines along its length at **235 mm** and **835 mm** from the same long edge.
Cut along both lines. Label the middle **600 × 1280 mm** piece **Long seat** and one **235 × 1280 mm** strip **Wall
backrest**.

On the remaining **235 × 1280 mm** strip, mark **580 mm** from one end. Cut across it to make **Right backrest, 235 ×
580 mm**, and **Front backrest, 235 × 700 mm**.

Hold the blade perpendicular to the foam surface. Guide it along the mark without squashing the foam. For foam deeper
than the blade reaches, cut partway, turn the foam over and finish from the matching line. Measure the relaxed pieces,
record any difference from the table, and lay all five in the van before cutting fabric.

### Wash a sample and check your tape and markings

Use the chosen fabric's care instructions. Measure a swatch, wash and dry it using those settings, and measure it again.
Check its feel and finish. Wash and dry the cover fabric before cutting if the supplier approves that process. Measure
its usable width afterwards.

Mark the wrong side of a scrap with your chosen chalk or removable marker. Remove the mark using its instructions. Stick
**3 mm sewing basting tape** to two scraps, peel off the backing and press them together. Check they stay aligned while
you handle them. For the cover seams, place tape **2–5 mm from the raw edge**, inside the 15 mm allowance. Keep the
stitch line clear of adhesive; [Sailrite explains needle gumming](https://www.sailrite.com/basting-tape-needle-gumming).

### Set the first cover's fit allowance

For the first trial, reduce each foam piece's length and width by **10 mm total**, leaving the height at **100 mm**. On
the right backrest, that gives seam-to-seam cover dimensions **570 × 225 × 100 mm** around foam **580 × 235 × 100 mm**.
You will adjust this allowance after fitting that cover.

[Sailrite's cushion sizing guide](https://www.fabric-calculator.com/boxcushions/eBook.htm) uses a snug relationship
between foam and cover. The 10 mm deduction here is our starting pattern choice for your trial. Your existing foam's
compressibility and the chosen fabric determine whether to keep it.

Add **15 mm seam allowance at each edge** after applying that fit deduction. The cut table below includes those
allowances. If you change the deduction after the trial, revise the table before cutting the remaining covers.

### Write the fabric cut list for all five covers

The table uses **Z = 30 mm**. If yours differs, change each zip-blank height from 160 to **130 + Z mm**. If your foam
differs from 100 mm thick, change each solid side height to **actual thickness + 30 mm** and each zip-blank height to
**actual thickness + 30 + Z mm**. Recheck the layout too.

### Mark and cut the first cover's fabric pieces

Lay the cloth flat with its wrong side up. Mark the first cover's rectangles from the cut table using a ruler and
square. Label each piece **Right backrest**, then **top**, **bottom**, **solid side**, **zip side**, **short side 1**,
**short side 2**, or **pull pocket**. Mark the intended zip-closing end.

Inside each structural raw edge, draw a line **15 mm** from that edge. Mark the intersections at the corners and mark
the midpoint of each edge. Cut along the outer rectangle with fabric scissors. Keep the inner lines for sewing. On the
pocket, mark the 80 mm direction as running along the zip.

### Zigzag the cut edges on the sewing machine

Fit the ordinary zigzag foot. On a scrap of your cloth, set **zigzag width 3 mm and length 2 mm**. Guide it so the
right-hand swing passes just beyond the raw edge and the left-hand swing catches the cloth. Sew 100 mm and inspect it.
If the edge curls, reduce the upper-thread tension a little and repeat on the scrap; retain the setting that leaves it
flat.

Zigzag around each cut panel, keeping its marked 15 mm stitch lines intact. After cutting the folded zip seam open in
the next step, zigzag each of its two new raw edges separately. Keep those allowances apart as you sew them.

### Sew the centre seam that will conceal the zip

Take the right-backrest zip blank, **600 × 160 mm**. Fold it along its length, right sides together, bringing the two
600 mm raw edges together. With **Z = 30 mm**, draw a line **15 mm from the folded edge**. For another Z, use half that
measured width here.

Mark points **35 mm from both short ends**. Set straight stitch length **3 mm** and sew the first 35 mm on that line,
backstitching two or three stitches at both ends. Sew the middle section with **4 mm temporary stitches**. Sew the last
35 mm at **3 mm stitch length**, securing its ends. Keep the middle temporary stitches free of tape.

Cut along the original folded edge without cutting the seam. Open out the fabric and spread the two seam allowances on
its wrong side. Finger-press them flat. Zigzag their cut edges. Measure the opened panel: it should be **600 × 130 mm**.
For each larger cover, use its own table length with this same cross-section.

### Fit the slider and position the zip

Use chain with its matching slider fitted. For loose sliders, follow [Mollies' slider-fitting
demonstration](https://www.mollies.nz/blogs/mollies-diy-blog/how-to-install-a-zipper-head): feed both tape ends into the
slider's wide end, keep them level and draw the slider along until the coil meshes. Keep it away from the ends until you
have sewn stops.

Close the right-backrest's **650 mm** chain. Put the panel wrong side up and the zip **pull face down against the
fabric**. Centre the coil over the temporary seam, with **25 mm of chain extending beyond each short end**. Check that
the pull will face outside when you open the cover's seam.

### Tape and sew the zip to the panel

Put 3 mm basting tape along the outer margins of the zip tape. Peel the backing and stick the zip onto the opened seam
allowances, keeping the coil centred over the seam. Leave the planned stitch lines clear of adhesive.

Fit the standard zipper foot. Set straight stitch to **3 mm**. Sew through the fabric and zip tape down each side of the
coil, starting about **6 mm from the centre seam**. Check underneath that both lines catch the woven zip tape.
Backstitch at each end. Adjust that spacing on the practice zip if your tape width requires it.

To pass the slider, stop with the needle in the fabric, lift the foot, move the slider out of the way, then lower the
foot and continue. Keep the needle clear of the slider.

### Remove the temporary stitches and sew zip stops

Use a seam ripper to remove the **4 mm basting stitches between the two 35 mm marks**. Leave the permanent end seams and
the two lines holding the zip intact. Remove the loose thread and run the slider through the opening.

Park the slider between the marks. Thread a hand needle with doubled polyester thread. At each mark, make **8–10 passes
over the closed coil**, putting the needle through the woven tape on either side. Knot the thread. Pull the slider
against each stop; add passes if it can ride over one. Trim the excess chain level with the panel's short ends after
both stops hold. The right-backrest opening is about **530 mm** long.

### Hem the pocket that will hide the pull

Take the **80 × 130 mm** pocket. On one 130 mm edge, turn **10 mm** to the wrong side, then turn another **10 mm**. Hold
the fold with basting tape clear of the stitch line. With the ordinary foot and 3 mm straight stitch, sew **2 mm inside
the inner fold**. You now have a pocket **60 × 130 mm** with one hemmed edge.

Place it right side up over the right side of the zip panel, at the end where the pull parks when closed. Match its
three raw edges to the panel's short end, top and bottom. Point the hemmed opening towards the middle of the zip. Slide
the pull under it to check the pocket covers it.

### Hold the pocket in place for the cushion seams

Tape the pocket's three raw edges inside the panel's 15 mm seam allowances. Leave the hemmed mouth free so you can reach
the pull. The side, top and bottom seams will secure those three edges while you assemble the cover.

Open and close the zip and tuck the pull under the pocket. Its depth after the corner seam will be about **45 mm**.
[Sailrite's cushion book, chapter
8](https://www.fabric-calculator.com/boxcushionexpress/Box%20Cushion%20Fabrication%20copy.pdf#page=53) shows another
pull-pocket construction using a fold in the side panel.

### Sew the four sides into a ring

Arrange solid long side, short side 1, zip side and short side 2 around the top face. Put neighbouring ends right sides
together. Align their top and bottom raw edges. On the right-backrest cover, each panel is **130 mm high**.

At each join, sew **15 mm from the short raw end**, starting **15 mm below the top** and stopping **15 mm above the
bottom**. Backstitch at both ends. Join all four corners, closing the ring. Finger-press those seam allowances apart.
Keep their unsewn top and bottom ends free for attaching the faces.

### Tape the top face to the side ring

Put the top face right side up. Place the ring against its edges with right sides together. Match the piece labels, edge
midpoints and four marked 15 mm corner intersections.

Tape one edge at a time inside the allowance. At each corner, fold the neighbouring side's loose allowance away from the
edge you are about to sew. Match the marked seam intersections. If the lengths differ, check the cut sizes and corner
marks before stitching.

### Sew the top face to the ring

Fit the ordinary foot and set straight stitch to **3 mm**. Sew each edge on its **15 mm line**, starting at one marked
corner intersection and ending at the next. Backstitch two or three stitches at the beginning and end. Support the loose
fabric on the table and let the machine feed it.

At each corner, stop and reposition the loose allowances before sewing the next edge. Keep the adjacent side out from
under the needle. Check that the two stitch lines meet at the same intersection and leave no corner hole. Inspect the
first corner for trapped cloth and a gap between the stitch lines before sewing the other three.

### Open the zip and attach the bottom face

Open the zip at least halfway. Match the bottom face's labels to the top, then tape it to the ring, right sides
together. Match the edge midpoints and marked corner intersections.

Sew its four edges at **15 mm**, using the same corner-to-corner sequence as the top. Keep the opposite face and loose
side fabric out of each seam. Leave the zip open so you can turn the cover through it.

### Turn the cover right side out

Inspect the inside seams for missed layers and corner holes. Repair those seams before turning the cover. Reach through
the zip and pull the cloth through. Push out the corners with your fingers or the rounded end of a smooth wooden spoon.

Open and close the zip. Check you can reach its pull under the pocket. Remove loose threads and visible tape. Keep the
corner allowances intact for the first fitting.

### Insert the foam

Open the zip to its stops. Compress the right-backrest foam with your hands and feed it through the opening. Work the
cover over the far corners, then over the near corners. Move the fabric over the foam a little at a time. Keep the
foam's bonded layers aligned.

If the cloth grips the foam, wrap the foam in a clean thin plastic sheet with one end left accessible at the zip. Slide
the cover over it, then pull the plastic out. Smooth the foam into the corners with flat hands.

### Check this cover before making the other four

Compress the foam by hand to bring the zip sides together, then close the zip. Check for a flat foam block, filled
corners, smooth faces and seams sitting at the edges. Remove and refit the cover once.

If the zip needs force or the foam bows, check your actual foam measurements and 15 mm seams. If those match, reduce the
10 mm fit deduction: try **5 mm total** on the next pattern, or **zero** if the foam barely compresses. A shallower seam
can add room, but it also leaves less cloth beside the stitches. Check that alteration with the tutor before recutting
the panel.

If the cloth bags, check for stretched fabric or seams shallower than 15 mm. Record the correction and revise all five
cut-list rows before cutting more. Keep the successful first cover beside you while sewing the remaining four.

### Fit all five cushions in the van

Arrange the pieces as seats and backrests, then make the **1070 × 1860 mm** bed using the layout in 2.1.5. Put on your
fitted sheet. Lie across the joins and sit on each seat. Check that you can lift the seats, reach the zips and remove
the covers.

Use the slatted bed base already in the design. Lift the cushions after sleeping and let the undersides dry before
packing bedding against them. Check the underside for dampness during the first few nights; [Sailrite's camper/boat
condensation guide](https://www.sailrite.com/How-to-Prevent-Cushion-Condensation) explains the need for airflow under
foam.

### Record the pattern and washing settings

Record each cushion's actual foam dimensions, final fit deduction, cut pieces, zip position, needle, machine model and
stitch settings. Keep the fabric care instructions and a labelled scrap with this guide.

Remove the foam before washing the covers. Use the settings checked on your sample and dry the cover before refitting
it. Update 2.2 with the cloth and zip quantities you used and the amounts you paid.

### What this means for your five cushions

Your cut-up mattress and convertible benches match the first two van builds. **Washable cotton duck/drill/canvas and
washable cotton/polyester upholstery fabric** have direct owner examples for this job. The boat reports add
service-history evidence for cotton drill, polycotton and ordinary upholstery cloth.

For the current NZ comparison, the cotton duck and canvas rows match a documented fabric family. The Moreland polyester
rows match the broader upholstery route, but still lack washing instructions. None of the owner reports used those exact
NZ products. The cotton listings also leave stain resistance unverified.

A cotton cover will need laundering after spills that soak into it. The fitted sheet covers the bed at night; the covers
remain exposed when you use the benches. Keep both washing and seat-surface feel in the comparison. The owner's choice
of a fibre alone cannot establish a particular cloth's stain resistance or seam strength.

Vinyl is another documented route, including the NZ build above. Its construction and cleaning differ from the woven,
washable covers in the current sewing steps. It remains an example in this research, with no added purchase row.
