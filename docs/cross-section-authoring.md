## Complete physical description

Use fixed-window side and sliding-door side, because opposite-side photographs reverse image left and right.
The pillar has a broad, relatively flat cabin-facing surface, a distinct edge on each side, and different
transitions toward its two windows. Photos show these surface-direction changes more clearly than the scan.

1. **Broad cabin-facing face.** A substantial painted strip between the fixed-window return and black sliding-door
   seal. It is the lighter grey face carrying the circular pressed feature in the closeup and the broad strip
   beside the black seal in the opposite view. It is substantially flat across its width, with comparatively even
   illumination and stronger brightness changes near its edges. It is wide enough to remain a recognisable face.
   The circular pressed feature is a local depression, with its own small rim and shadow, not the pillar profile.
   Face boundaries run mainly vertically and change gradually with height near the roof and lower body. That
   vertical shaping is distinct from a horizontal cross-section through the face.
2. **Fixed-window-side edge.** A clear longitudinal direction change between the light broad face and darker
   return toward the covered window. A relatively narrow highlight/shading band marks the turn. Both adjoining
   surfaces remain distinct. Formed sheet metal gives local rounding, not rounding across entire adjacent faces.
   The photos do not establish its numerical radius.
3. **Darker recessed return.** A separate surface of visible breadth and depth between the broad face's edge and
   the region beside the window covering. Its orientation is shown by shading and vertically continuing boundaries.
   Much of its width appears comparatively straight; strongest curvature is near its boundaries. At lower heights,
   its relationship to the surround changes as that surround sweeps around the window's lower corner. Do not merge
   face, return and surround into a ramp or insert an unsupported generic step.
4. **Fixed-window flare/surround.** Beyond the return the metal transitions outward to the recessed glazing. The
   formed surround separates pillar face and glass and follows the window perimeter. Its larger sweep around the
   lower window corner differs from the small local rounding at the pillar edge. Keep both kinds of curvature
   distinct. White covering and tape obscure the glass boundary and do not measure metal profile or glass depth.
5. **Sliding-opening-side edge.** The other edge of the broad face approaches a narrow painted region beside the
   seal. There is a distinct boundary between stationary pillar and door; the broad face does not gradually run
   into the sliding-door glass. Rubber conceals some of the metal turn. Visible paint/rubber edges are not
   automatically the exact endpoints of the underlying metal fold.
6. **Black rubber seal.** A separate substantial, ribbed strip at the opening. It runs vertically, bends around
   the relatively tight upper corner and continues across the opening's top. That turn differs from the more
   expansive painted door-surround shaping. Preserve its separate thickness, ribbing and rounded exterior rather
   than absorbing it into a continuous metal profile. Visible seal contour does not reveal hidden metal shape.
7. **Sliding door's own window surround.** Beyond the seal is the door's separate painted inner structure: a broad
   silver surround slopes toward the glass and wraps around its rounded corners. Below, it broadens and curves
   into the lower door panel; above, it has a continuous formed transition around the glazing. Its flare is not
   the stationary pillar's seal-side flank. These belong to different physical components.
8. **Upper junction.** The pillar widens and blends into roof-side structure. The opening seal turns across the top
   while painted metal spreads into the roof rail. This has broader three-dimensional shaping than mid-pillar.
   Do not copy that curvature down the pillar or round the whole middle horizontal section.

## Required numerical method

1. Record every feature and its evidence: position, direction, extent and source for each face, flare, return,
   corner and seal boundary. Distinguish measurements, photo-supported reconstructions and unresolved quantities.
   Preserve all details rather than reducing the pillar to convenient points.
2. Establish registered coordinates and Z=991mm. Check measurement heights and endpoints. The 180mm pillar width
   at another height is a constraint to investigate, not a width to transplant.
3. Identify the broad face's two edges, return/surround boundary and seal contact in the photos, then locate their
   corresponding registered scan texture features at this same section height. Retain positional uncertainty.
   Green contour curvature does not determine folds. Texture-screen mapping is valid only for actual same-plane
   intersections; camera projection and foreshortening must be accounted for.
4. Establish glazing planes, broad-face plane and each return surface independently from evidence belonging to that
   surface. Where a sufficiently intact scan patch matches a photographed flat face, check selected separated
   observations for consistency. Exclude smoothed edge bands, seals and neighbouring surfaces. No point averaging
   or automatic contour fitting.
5. Intersect adjoining surfaces only once their directions and positions are supported; check candidate sharp folds
   against photographed edge locations. Intersecting arbitrary lines through a rounded scan does not qualify.
6. Constrain missing depths/angles using both views, supported surface locations and applicable measurements.
   Photo pixel widths alone are not millimetres; account for perspective and foreshortening. Retain supported
   ranges when multiple constructions remain possible rather than silently selecting one.
7. Establish faces/junctions first, then constrain local rounds from suitably resolved scaled views or measurements.
   Scan smoothing radius is not metal corner radius. Keep hidden metal under the seal as a separate uncertainty.
8. Compare the complete construction against both photos, scan texture, applicable dimensions and neighbouring
   sections. Check every agreed feature and the separate pillar, rubber and door. Reflect supported geometry across
   the centreline. Gert visually approves the result before recording.

Best match means satisfying independent evidence while preserving photographed construction, not minimising
error to green. Before changing an underdetermined dimension, identify that specific quantity and its supported
bounds. Unsupported values from earlier rejected drafts are not evidence. Do not claim a correction is complete
merely because it saved, rendered or passed schema validation.
