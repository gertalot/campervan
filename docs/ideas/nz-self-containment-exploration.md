## Official use-space guidance

PGDB inspection guidance v1.1, pages 12–13, Figures 1–2 recommends:

- 650 mm overall lateral clearance, with 325 mm from bowl centre to either side.
- At least 350 mm forward clearance.
- At least 1 m from toilet seat to ceiling.

The guidance permits slightly smaller clearances where usability is established. It also allows appropriately fixed
heavy-duty rail arrangements in principle, subject to the other requirements. Its toilet section says usable within the
vehicle; it does not explicitly state bed-down use. We retain bed-down use as the owner's design requirement.

[Official PGDB inspection guidance](https://www.pgdb.co.nz/media/cqubmqa3/vehicle-inspection-guidance-v11.pdf)

## Implications for the agreed 150 × 186 cm layout

The 110 cm extended bed leaves a 40 cm strip. The small toilet bodies fit that width on paper, before cabinetry and
mounting allowances. That does not prove shoulder, leg, lid or emptying access. A full-height 40 cm enclosure would be
narrower than the official recommended lateral use space. Nearby low furniture might allow upper-body clearance,
but that remains an unverified design possibility.

The strongest established precedent reserves space near a sliding door/front of the bed. For this van, exploring that
would require redistributing part of the bed or cabinetry, or proving usable space beyond the 186 cm rectangle. No such
space is assumed available. Another branch is an approved fixed slide-out arrangement, but its deployed position must
remain usable with the bed extended; merely stowing it below the bed does not solve night access.

No toilet, new bed shape, water system or self-contained layout selected.

## Follow-up: toilets fixed on sliders

Checked 14 October 2026. PGDB guidance section 2.3 explicitly accommodates toilets appropriately fixed on heavy-duty
rails, provided the base remains rigidly mounted and does not need removal to empty waste. Adjustability alone does
not disqualify a toilet. The installation must still meet the other requirements.

More specific evidence: NZ Lifestyle Camping issued a conditional approval dated 18 October 2024 for Myst's
Fix'n'Rail base. Conditions include permanent rigid mounting of the base; the toilet firmly mounted and bolted to it;
removable waste container accessible without spillage; sealed, cleanable surrounding surfaces; adequate waste capacity;
and easy access and use inside the vehicle. This is an authority's conditional endorsement, not unconditional approval
of every toilet/rail combination or of this van.

[NZ Lifestyle Camping approval letter](https://www.mystenterprise.com/shop/fixnrailtm-toilet-bracket-356/document/92)

Kiwi's installation guide calls for sliding assemblies to lock securely in their stored and usable positions, and for
the waste-emptying procedure to leave the mounted base attached. Myst's Fix'n'Rail listing also describes a locking
slide. Its published bracket dimensions must not be mistaken for the installed toilet-plus-bracket envelope.

[Kiwi installation guide](https://www.kiwiselfcontained.co.nz/blogs/news/how-to-install-fix-a-potty-bracket)
[Fix'n'Rail product and documents](https://www.mystenterprise.com/shop/fixnrailtm-toilet-bracket-356)

For the agreed 45 cm mattress-base height, a roughly 30–33.5 cm-high toilet body is a plausible under-bed candidate.
That is a packaging inference: runners, tray, bed framing, clear opening and exact toilet dimensions must be added.
The toilet can occupy an under-bed compartment in storage and a different position when used, while remaining attached.
This saves daytime space but still requires a usable deployed position with the bed extended. The previous drawings
show deployment envelopes; they need not be occupied by the toilet throughout the day.

PGDB section 4.3 also clarifies that removable toilet cassettes are exempt from the direct-to-exterior wastewater-tank
vent requirement. General vehicle ventilation and any applicable manufacturer instructions still matter.

## Correction and research: practical minimum use space

The earlier 65 × 77 cm figure for a 37 × 42 cm toilet was a conservative planning rectangle derived from recommended
clearances. It is not a mandatory clear-floor minimum. The owner's priority is a tightly optimised microcamper layout.
No fixed 65 cm floor aisle or full-height empty rectangle is adopted as a requirement.

Targeted research covered NZ hire/conversion listings, installation products, original DIY diaries, owner forums and
hands-on vehicle reviews. No inspected source supplies a measured smaller complete width-by-length use envelope for
a current NZ green-certified NV200 toilet installation. This is a research limit, not a reason to assume a large cubicle.

### Narrow dimensions with explicit evidence

- [Modiwell R1808 manufacturer](https://modiwell.com/products/modiwell-elite-r1808-rv-toilet-waterless-dry-flush-fixed)
  lists a 14.4 inch (36.6 cm) body width and recommended space width at least 15.7 inches (39.9 cm). It does not publish
  the person's complete front-clearance envelope. Different toilet and no verified NZ approval; not a purchase proposal.
- [Dormobile Ballintrae hands-on review](https://www.outandaboutlive.co.uk/reviews/campervans/dormobile-ballintrae-high-top-rising-roof-campervan)
  measures 42 cm between wall and worktop at shoulder level. The reviewer reports needing to lean forward or sit at an
  angle. This documents actual constrained use, not a comfortable universal minimum; larger vehicle, different toilet.

### Direct owner and builder experience

- [NV200 owner's bed/sofa diary](https://nv200campervan.wordpress.com/2015/11/03/bedsofa/): shortened one person's bed
  side by approximately one foot (30.5 cm) to enable nighttime porta-potti use; reports needing agility. That number is
  removed bed length, not a measured front clearance. Older UK portable setup, no NZ green certification evidence.
- [John Johnson's toilet modification](https://www.truckcampermagazine.com/camper-mods/contests/camper-toilet-turned-for-more-leg-room/):
  original toilet required opening the door for a knee. Rotating it 90 degrees to face the existing shower space solved
  the problem. Supports sharing leg space and choosing orientation; publishes no transferable use-space minimum.
- [NZ Fix-a-Potty customer reports](https://fixapotty.co.nz/product/portable-toilet-bracket/): Toyota Estima owners
  Daniel and Blanka @WLCampers report retaining their existing arrangement without extra space and green compliance.
  Other customers explicitly report receiving green warrants. Supplier-hosted testimonials, with no use-space dimensions.
- [VanLab NZ NV200 Small](https://vanlab.co.nz/small): maker says its toilet slides from under the front bench and
  can be used with the bed set up. No measured use envelope or verified current green-warrant status in inspected page.

### Verified storage allowance

[Kiwi's complete 10L sliding kit](https://www.kiwiselfcontained.co.nz/products/10l-fix-a-potty-sliding-toilet-kit)
has body dimensions 35 W × 41.5 D × 30 H cm. Supplier requires 10 cm behind the stored toilet, making the minimum
stored depth approximately 51.5 cm; runners are 40 cm long and 1.2 cm high. This is specific hardware geometry,
not seated-use clearance, and cannot automatically be applied to a perpendicular-sliding custom tray.

### Design implication

Investigate retaining a 40–45 cm floor corridor with the toilet facing along it and nearby low furniture, instead of
automatically widening the corridor to 65 cm. This is a candidate to test, not an established compliant minimum.
The fore-aft space and elbows above adjacent furniture remain unresolved. Do not invent 20/25 cm front clearance.
EPIC's cabin width minus its tapered bed width does not establish toilet clearance: trim, furniture, and the front-door
position prevent that subtraction from proving the geometry. No approved layout or mattress cut changes.

## Follow-up: fore-aft length with a 40 cm-wide open aisle

Owner now adopts 40 cm floor width provisionally, with elbow space above the bed. Research excludes enclosed bathrooms
as dimensional comparators. The truck-camper bathroom example above is withdrawn from justification for this layout.

New directly relevant original build evidence:

- [Ryan and Mel, NV200 under-bed toilet](https://www.doyourdream.co.uk/2016/07/nv200-under-bed-storage-and-toilet/)
  explicitly report a low toilet is tight but fully usable with the bed down. Their specification names a Fiamma Bi-Pot
  30. Inspected photos show storage and deployed positions, but no seated person or measured clearance ahead of the bowl.
  This is an older UK portable setup, not evidence of current NZ fixed-toilet certification.
- [Carla Watkins, Toyota Auris microcamper](https://colourfulmagicalweirdo.substack.com/p/girl-meets-van-ii-fitting-a-toilet)
  reports successful nighttime use of a toilet in the rear passenger footwell alongside the bed, accessed by rotating
  from the bed. She says only one orientation works and moves the front passenger seat forwards. No footwell dimensions
  are published. A directly relevant open compact-space use report, but different toilet and vehicle.

These support compact open arrangements and the importance of orientation, not a specific 20/25/30 cm front minimum.
For the owner's 42 cm-deep toilet, 77 cm total remains the sourced conservative starting length (42 + recommended35).
At adopted width40, this is a 40 × 77 cm provisional floor plan, not an established legal or ergonomic minimum.
No reliable complete shorter fore-aft use dimension was established by this additional research.
